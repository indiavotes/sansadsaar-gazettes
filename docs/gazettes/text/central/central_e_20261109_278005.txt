Subject: Initiation of anti-dumping investigation concerning imports of “Aluminium Extrusion Profiles, 
Structures and Articles thereof” from China PR, Malaysia, Vietnam and Thailand. 


A. Background 


F. No. 6/67/2026-DGTR.—Having regard to the Customs Tariff Act, 1975, as amended from time to time 
(hereinafter referred to as the ‘Act’) and the Customs Tariff (Identification, Assessment and Collection of Anti- 
Dumping Duty on Dumped Articles and for Determination of Injury) Rules, 1995, as amended from time to time 
(hereinafter referred to as the ‘AD Rules, 1995’ or the ‘Rules’), representations have been received by the Designated 
Authority (hereinafter referred to as the ‘Authority’) from the Aluminium Extrusion Manufacturers Association of 
India (hereinafter referred to as ‘ALEMAI’ or the ‘Association’), representing 228 manufacturers of “Aluminium 
Extrusion Profiles, Structures and Articles thereof” (hereinafter referred to as the ‘product under consideration’ or the 
‘subject goods’ or the ‘PUC’). ALEMAT has stated that the domestic industry is suffering injury on account of a 
significant increase in the dumped imports of the subject goods from China PR, Malaysia, Vietnam and Thailand 
(hereinafter referred to as subject countries). 


1. Nine domestic producers, who are members of ALEMAI, have also filed representations seeking imposition of 
ADD. These companies have furnished their financial statements Additionally, vide letters dated 4" November 
2026, 3 domestic producers who are members of ALEMAI have submitted summary cost information, and 
estimates of dumping and injury margin in respect of imports from the subject countries.

ALEMAT has submitted that most of its members are small units in the MSME category, and has requested the

The Authority has examined the information furnished by ALEMAI and its member producers, along with the

Rule 5(4) of the Rules provides as under: 
“(4) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-rule (1) the designated authority may initiate an 
investigation suo motu if it is satisfied from the information received from the Principal Commissioner of 
Customs or Commissioner of Customs, as the case may be, appointed under the Customs Act, 1962 (52 of

referred to in clause (b) of sub-rule (3).” 


The Authority, accordingly, takes cognizance of the information provided by ALEMAI and its member 
producers and the import data available on record, in terms of Rule 5(4) of the Rules, for the purpose of 
examining whether sufficient evidence exists as to the existence of dumping, injury and causal link, as referred 
to in Rule 5(3)(b) of the Rules. 


B. Product under consideration 


The product under consideration in the present investigation is “Aluminium Extrusion Profiles, Structures and 
Articles thereof’. The subject goods may be alloyed or non-alloyed and are produced by the extrusion process 
in the form of profiles (solid, hollow or semi-hollow), bars, rods, tubes and pipes. 


The subject goods are sold either in mill finish or surface treated (anodized, powder coated, liquid painted, 
electrophoretically coated, wood-grain or sublimation finished, polished or brushed). The goods may be sold as 
such, or further fabricated (i.e., cut to length, drilled, punched, notched, mitred, bent, welded, machined or 
threaded), and are used to make structures and articles for eventual end consumption. 


Accordingly, the scope of the product under consideration includes the following: 

(a) Extruded profiles for architectural, industrial, transport, electrical and general engineering applications; 

(b) Extruded bars and rods, other than wire rods; 

(c) Extruded seamed or seamless tubes and pipes, whether or not subsequently drawn; 

(d) Doors, windows and their frames and thresholds, curtain-wall and fagade systems, partitions, louvres, 
railings and balustrades, and other structures and parts of structures made from aluminium extrusions, as well 
as profiles, tubes and the like prepared for use in structures; and 

(e) Other articles made predominantly from aluminium extrusions, such as heat sinks, LED and lighting 
profiles, industrial framing (T-slot) profiles, solar mounting structures, ladders and furniture, kitchen and 
wardrobe profiles, bicycle parts and formwork. 


ALEMAT has submitted that profiles, bars, rods, tubes, structures and articles made through the extrusion 
process are different variants of a single product, since (a) all forms are produced from aluminium billet, cast 
from primary aluminium ingot and/or aluminium scrap with the required alloying material; (b) all forms are 
produced on the same hydraulic extrusion press, through the same stages of billet heating, extrusion, 
quenching, stretching, cutting and ageing, the only difference between a hollow and a solid profile being the 
die; (c) structures and articles are made from extruded profiles only by cutting, machining, welding or joining, 
and the extruded profile gives the finished structure or article its essential character and accounts for the 
predominant part of its cost; and (d) the output at each stage (profile, structure or article) is capable of being 
sold as such.

The Authority prima facie notes that the structures and articles included in the scope are made from extruded 
profiles by cutting, machining, welding or joining. Such processes are incremental, and no additional raw 
material, plant or primary process is required to produce the structures and articles. Accordingly, for the 
purpose of initiation, aluminium profiles, structures and articles made through the extrusion process have been 
considered as part of the product under consideration. The scope shall be examined further in light of the 
comments received from the interested parties. 


The product under consideration is used in a wide range of architectural, industrial, transport, electrical and 
general engineering applications, such as windows, doors, curtain-wall and facade systems, partitions, railings, 
heat sinks and lighting profiles, industrial framing, solar mounting structures, ladders, furniture, kitchen and 
wardrobe profiles, bicycle parts and formwork. 


The main raw material is aluminium billet, made by melting primary aluminium and/or aluminium scrap and 
adding small quantities of other metals to obtain the required alloy. The billet is cut to length, heated to about 
450-500°C and pushed by a hydraulic ram through a die. The extruded profile is cooled, stretched, cut to 
standard lengths and aged in an oven to attain the required hardness and strength (such as T5 or T6 temper). 
The profiles may thereafter be anodised, powder coated or otherwise finished, and may be cut, drilled, 
punched, machined, welded or joined to make structures and articles.

D. Domestic industry and standing 


The Authority notes that the Indian industry producing the subject goods is fragmented in nature and comprises 
largely of units in the MSME category. ALEMAIT represents 228 manufacturers of the subject goods in India. 
The Authority is prima facie satisfied that the representation has been made by or on behalf of the domestic 
industry in terms of the provisions contained in Rule 2(b) and Rule 5(3) of the Rules. 


Considering the MSME and fragmented nature of the industry and the number of producers involved, the 
Authority, in terms of Trade Notice 09/2021 dated 297 July 2021, seeks information from the domestic 
producers as per Annexure I referred to in the said trade notice. Based on the information so received, the 
Authority shall determine the scope of the domestic industry within the meaning of Rule 2(b) of the Rules and 
shall undertake sampling of domestic producers for the purpose of determination of injury margin. Therefore, 
while the information concerning all producers constituting the domestic industry will be considered for injury 
analysis, the non-injurious price and injury margin will be determined based on the data of the sampled 
producers. 


E. Subject countries 


The subject countries in the present investigation are China PR, Malaysia, Vietnam and Thailand (hereinafter 
referred to as the ‘subject countries’). 


F. Period of investigation 


The period of investigation (‘POI’) for the present investigation is Ist April 2025 to 30th June 2026 (15 
months). The injury investigation period shall cover the periods Ist April 2022 to 31st March 2023, Ist April 
2023 to 31st March 2024, Ist April 2024 to 31st March 2025 and the POI. 


Rule 5(3A) of the Rules provides that the period of investigation shall not be more than six months old as on 
the date of initiation, and shall normally be twelve months, while for reasons to be recorded in writing the 
Authority may consider a minimum of six months or a maximum of eighteen months. In the present case, the 
domestic producers are predominantly MSME units which maintain their records on a financial-year basis. 
Adoption of a twelve-month period from July 2025 to June 2026 would have resulted in substantial practical 
difficulties in the preparation and verification of information by such producers. Inclusion of the complete 
financial year 2025-26 within the POI is, therefore, considered appropriate, and is not expected to skew the 
data. Accordingly, a period of investigation of fifteen months has been adopted in the present case.

H. Basis of alleged injury 


ALEMAT has made representations before the Authority that the domestic industry in India has suffered injury 
due to the dumped imports into the country. The volume of the subject imports from the subject countries has 
increased significantly in absolute terms. Available information also indicates that the industry has suffered 
injury as the dumped imports were undercutting the prices of the industry, resulting in significant decline in 
profitability, and it is suffering losses. The industry is also facing decline in cash profits and return on capital 
employed. The industry is also facing significant underutilization of production capacities. While complete 
information on the impact on economic parameters is not available with the Authority at this stage, it is 
nonetheless prima facie observed based on information received that the domestic industry has suffered injury 
due to dumped imports. 


I. Initiation of suo motu anti-dumping investigation 


In view of the foregoing, and having satisfied itself, on the basis of the information furnished by ALEMAI and 
its member producers and the import data available on record, that sufficient evidence exists as to the dumping 
of the product under consideration originating in or exported from the subject countries, the consequent 
material injury to the domestic industry and the causal link between such injury and the dumped imports, as 
referred to in Rule 5(3)(b) of the Rules, the Authority, in accordance with Section 9A of the Act read with Rule 
5(4) of the Rules, hereby suo motu initiates an anti-dumping investigation to determine the existence, degree 
and effect of the alleged dumping in respect of the product under consideration originating in or exported from 
the subject countries, and to recommend the amount of anti-dumping duty which, if levied, would be adequate 
to remove the injury to the domestic industry.

M. Submission of information on confidential basis 


Where any party to the present investigation makes confidential submissions or provides information on a 
confidential basis before the Authority, such party is required to simultaneously submit a non-confidential 
version of such information in terms of Rule 7(2) of the Rules and in accordance with the relevant trade notices 
issued by the Authority in this regard. Failure to adhere to the above may lead to rejection of the 
response/submissions. 


The parties making any submission (including Appendices/Annexes attached thereto) before the Authority, 
including questionnaire responses, are required to file confidential and non-confidential versions separately. 


Such submissions must be clearly marked as ‘confidential’ or ‘non-confidential’ at the top of each page. Any 
submission that has been made to the Authority without such markings shall be treated as ‘non-confidential’ 
information by the Authority, and the Authority shall be at liberty to allow other interested parties to inspect 
such submissions. 


The confidential version shall contain all information which is, by nature, confidential and/or other information 
which the supplier of such information claims as confidential. For the information which is claimed to be 
confidential by nature, or the information on which confidentiality is claimed for other reasons, the supplier of 
the information is required to provide a good cause statement along with the supplied information as to why 
such information cannot be disclosed. 


The non-confidential version of the information filed by the interested parties should be a replica of the 
confidential version, with the confidential information preferably indexed or blanked out (where indexation is 
not possible), and such information must be appropriately and adequately summarized depending upon the 
information on which confidentiality is claimed. 


The non-confidential summary must be in sufficient detail to permit a reasonable understanding of the 
substance of the information furnished on a confidential basis. However, in exceptional circumstances, the 
party submitting the confidential information may indicate that such information is not susceptible to summary, 
and a statement of reasons containing a sufficient and adequate explanation, in terms of Rule 7 of the Rules and 
the appropriate trade notices issued by the Authority, as to why such summarization is not possible, must be 
provided to the satisfaction of the Authority.

trade notices issued by the Authority, shall not be taken on record by the Authority.

information submitted. If the Authority is satisfied that the request for confidentiality is not warranted, or if the 
supplier of the information is either unwilling to make the information public or to authorise its disclosure in

The Authority, on being satisfied and accepting the need for confidentiality of the information provided, shall
