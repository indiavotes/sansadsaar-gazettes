Introduction 


1.1.Interconnection in telecommunications refers to commercial and technical arrangements that allow authorised 


2.1. 


3.1. 


entities or licensees to connect their telecommunication equipment, networks, and services, enabling their users 
to access the users, services, and networks of other authorized entities or licensees. 


The Register of Interconnect Agreements Regulations, 1999, were notified on 317 August 1999, and came into 
force on 17 September 1999, to establish a regulatory framework for registration of interconnect agreements 
entered into between service providers. These regulations prescribe the modalities for the maintenance of the 
register of interconnect agreements between service providers and matters connected therewith. These 
regulations cover important aspects relating to the submission and registration of interconnect agreements, 
maintenance of the register by the Authority, contents of the interconnect agreement to be kept confidential, 
inspection of interconnect agreements, and related processes. These regulations were subsequently amended 
on 3 February 2004, 31% December 2004, and 47 March 2005. 


This Explanatory Memorandum sets out the background, rationale, and considerations underlying the Register 
of Interconnect Agreements (Fourth Amendment) Regulations, 2026, to enable stakeholders to understand the 
Authority’s approach in formulating these regulations. 


Present exercise to review the regulatory framework for the register of interconnect agreements in India 


The Authority issued a pre-consultation paper dated 377 April 2025 and subsequently issued a consultation 
paper on the ‘Review of existing TRAI Regulations on Interconnection matters’ on 10 November 2025 to 
seek the views of stakeholders on different aspects of these Regulations. The stakeholders were requested to 
submit their written comments and counter-comments by the extended last dates of 157 December 2025 and 
297 December 2025, respectively. An open house discussion was held with the stakeholders on 287 January 
2026. The issues raised in the consultation paper, submissions of stakeholders through comments and counter- 
comments and during the open house discussion, have been examined in the succeeding paragraphs.

3.4.6. Many stakeholders submitted that the existing system of floppy disk and paper-based processes of 
submission of interconnect agreements and modifications thereto should be dispensed with, and 
needs to be transitioned to a fully digital, online framework. Stakeholders have emphasized that the 
current system is outdated and inefficient, and that it requires replacement with digital processes 
aligned with evolving technological and regulatory practices. Further, stakeholders have broadly 
supported the adoption of a centralized online portal to facilitate end-to-end digital processes, 
including submission, acknowledgment, tracking, inspection, and access to interconnect agreements. 


3.4.7. A few stakeholders have further highlighted the importance of adopting submission of an electronic 
copy of documents in place of wet-signed physical documents as submission of physical documents 
leads to delays and inefficiencies. 


3.4.8. Some stakeholders have also emphasized the need for submission of searchable documents in place 
of non-searchable scanned copies, as searchable scanned documents facilitate efficiency in reviewing 
and retrieving information therein. 


3.4.9. Regarding the timeline for submission, a few stakeholders proposed that electronic copies of 
interconnect agreements should be submitted through the TRAI portal within 15 or 7 days of 
execution. 


3.4.10. Regarding the fees for seeking inspection and for obtaining copies of the relevant portions of the 
Register, many stakeholders submitted that any associated fees should be nominal, reasonable, and 
clearly specified. 

3.4.11. A few stakeholders recommended facilitating public access to non-confidential records through 
electronic means only and dispensing with inspection and copying fees in the digital environment. 


3.4.12. Regarding the incorporation of provisions for necessary financial disincentives into interconnection 
regulations to deter non-compliance, some stakeholders stated that financial disincentives are not 
required, arguing that a mature telecom market and existing regulatory provisions are sufficient to 
ensure compliance without additional penalties. Other stakeholders argued in favour of incorporation 
of a necessary financial disincentive framework. One stakeholder stated that appropriate provisions 
for financial disincentives should be incorporated into the regulation to deter non-compliance by 
private and PSU operators alike. Other stakeholders recommended financial disincentives as a 
deterrent to ensure compliance with inter-operator rights and obligations. They emphasized that 
financial disincentives should be applied only in extreme cases, after giving service providers an 
opportunity for a hearing and conclusive proof of non-compliance.

The existing provisions requiring submission of paper-based agreements together with soft copies 
on 3.5” floppy/diskette are obsolete, technologically outdated, and no longer aligned with prevailing 
practices. The continued reliance on physical documentation and outdated storage media results in 
procedural inefficiencies. These inefficiencies can be avoided by online submissions and dispensing 
with the process of physical submissions of interconnect agreements. Accordingly, it is decided that 
submission of interconnect agreements and modifications thereto, under these regulations, shall 
henceforth be made only in electronic form through a portal. This framework is expected to facilitate 
streamlined submission, handling, maintenance, and retrieval of records, while reducing dependence 
on physical documentation and outdated technological formats. 


In order to ensure continued accessibility and convenience for the public, inspection of the register 
and obtaining extracts from the relevant portions thereof should be permitted in both physical and 
electronic modes through a portal. Enabling access through electronic mode, in addition to the 
existing physical mode, would provide greater flexibility, facilitate efficient retrieval and 
dissemination of information, and promote ease of access for users. Further, provisions governing 
the inspection of the register and obtaining extracts therefrom shall remain similar for both physical 
mode and electronic mode through a portal. 


It is observed that text-searchable electronic documents in standard formats, such as portable 
document format (PDF), would enhance ease of processing and efficiency in record management. 
Accordingly, it is decided that all service providers shall submit a text-searchable PDF copy of their 
interconnect agreements and modifications thereto on the TRAI portal within the period specified 
in these regulations. 


The proposal to shorten the timeline for submitting interconnect agreements has been examined. In 
this regard, some of the stakeholders have suggested a timeline of fifteen days from the date of 
execution of the interconnect agreement, whereas some other stakeholders have suggested a timeline 
of seven days. It is noted that the existing timeline of thirty days was prescribed at a time when the 
interconnect agreements were required to be submitted in physical form, along with a soft copy ina 
floppy or diskette. It has now been decided that the interconnect agreements shall be submitted in an 
online manner through a portal. Accordingly, the time needed for submission would be substantially 
reduced. Considering this, a timeline of fifteen days from the date of execution or modification of 
the interconnect agreement would be appropriate in an online environment. At the same time, the 
service providers will be free to submit the interconnect agreements earlier than the prescribed 
timeline, if they are in a position to do so. 


It is noted that while the Authority may specify formats for seeking disaggregated information 
relating to interconnect agreements under regulation 7, the existing provisions do not explicitly 
provide for the furnishing of such information within a specified timeframe. Accordingly, a provision 
has been inserted requiring service providers to furnish such information in the specified format and 
within the period specified by the Authority, so as to facilitate effective maintenance and timely 
updation of the Register.

The Authority has examined the comments received from stakeholders regarding the necessary 
financial disincentive framework in the Register of Interconnect Agreements Regulations, 1999, as 
amended from time to time. The financial disincentives play an important role in ensuring regulatory 
compliance by service providers, as well as in safeguarding the rights of service providers under the 
interconnection framework. 


It is decided that financial disincentives be imposed on a case-by-case basis, after providing the 
concerned service provider a reasonable opportunity to present its explanation. It is also decided that 
the financial disincentive framework be introduced to ensure proportionality and deterrence. The 
quantum of financial disincentive should be reasonable and balanced so as to serve as an effective 
deterrent against non-compliance, without being either negligible or unduly burdensome. 


The Authority has considered the nature and potential impact of non-compliance with the provisions 
of these regulations. The provisions covered under regulation 5 and regulation 6 relate to various 
aspects of submission of interconnect agreements and modifications to them for registration and 
inclusion in the Register. These requirements are important for keeping the Register complete, 
accurate and up to date. Any non-compliance with these regulations, i.e., failure to submit the 
interconnect agreement and modifications, within the period specified by the Authority, may directly 
and significantly affect the proper functioning of the interconnection framework. The Authority is, 
therefore, of the view that a higher financial disincentive is appropriate for non-compliance of 
regulation 5 or regulation 6. 


On the other hand, regulation 7 relates to furnishing of disaggregated information in the prescribed 
formats pertaining to interconnect agreements. This information is important for maintaining the
